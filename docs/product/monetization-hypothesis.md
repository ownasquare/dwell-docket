# Monetization hypothesis

## Hypothesis

Small carriers and dispatch teams may pay for a hosted version if it reliably
reduces the weekly effort needed to find and document missed detention. A
possible model is a low monthly team subscription with a free local
reconciliation tier.

Potential paid value could include saved private dockets, carrier-specific
rules, evidence attachment checklists, team assignment, aging reminders, and
export to an existing invoicing workflow. Each addition would need separate
validation and must not compromise the simple local core.

## What is supported

Current public pain and workaround evidence shows that detention waits,
proof disputes, deadline-sensitive claims, and abandoned small balances are
real problems. The repository proves only that the proposed one-file
transformation works locally.

## What is not proved

- Hosted proof: none.
- Production proof: none.
- Provider proof: none; no provider is integrated.
- Payment proof: none.
- Buyer or revenue proof: none.
- Usage proof: none beyond local automated validation.
- Demand proof: public problem evidence only; no interviews, waitlist,
  conversion, retention, or purchase intent.

No pricing, revenue, conversion, or viral-growth number is presented as a
forecast.

## Validation sequence before commercialization

1. Show the local workflow to independently recruited small-carrier
   dispatchers using synthetic data.
2. Confirm their existing export can be represented by the explicit schema
   without a mapping wizard.
3. Measure whether the docket reduces weekly review time and surfaces
   previously missed balances.
4. Test willingness to pay without collecting payment.
5. Only then evaluate hosted storage, authentication, integrations, and
   payment infrastructure as separate authorized work.
